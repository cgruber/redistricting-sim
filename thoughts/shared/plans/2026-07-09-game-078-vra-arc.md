# GAME-078 — VRA / Callais scenario arc (implementation plan)

## §GOAL

Author two new Educational-campaign scenarios that extend the game's Voting Rights Act
teaching from a single lesson (Valle Verde: *create* a majority-minority district to cure
vote dilution) into the genuine two-failure zone and its post-*Callais* successor:

- **Scenario A — "The 55% Problem"** (`scenario-010`): a district drawn *more* majority-minority
  than the law requires — by reaching across the county with a non-compact arm — can be struck
  down as an unconstitutional racial gerrymander (*Bethune-Hill v. Virginia*). The player
  **remediates** a court-struck over-packed map: redraw a *compact* opportunity district that
  still gives the community a majority. No new engine mechanics.
- **Scenario B — "The Proxy Problem"** (`scenario-011`): post-*Louisiana v. Callais* (2026),
  race-conscious redistricting is constitutionally suspect while partisan gerrymandering is
  non-justiciable (*Rucho*, 2019). The player must find and fairly represent the minority
  community using **only proxy data** (income, language, voting history) — the race column is
  hidden until a post-result reveal. Requires **new engine mechanics**.

This plan is the implementation design for GAME-078; it consumes DESIGN-013 (scenario design)
and applies the DESIGN-014 neutrality gate. It deliberately re-scopes DESIGN-013 where that
ticket is stale (numbering; the "functional threshold" criterion claim; placement math).

### Code reality that reshapes the design (verified this session)
- **`majority_minority` is a pure population-share threshold** (`evaluate.ts:341`): it counts
  districts where the filtered group's **population share** ≥ `min_eligible_share`, then checks
  `qualifying ≥ min_districts`. It does **not** model voting-age population (VAP/CVAP), racially
  polarized voting, or electoral outcome. DESIGN-013's criterion note ("functional threshold —
  minority voters can elect preferred candidate") **overclaims** what the sim computes. That
  "functional / can-elect" language must live in **prose as real-world context with the
  simplification named**, never as a claim about the mechanic.
- **`compactness` genuinely distinguishes snakes from blobs** (`evaluate.ts:82-115`): score =
  fraction of a district's hex-edge slots whose neighbour is in the same district (interior-edge
  fraction). Range 0 (singletons) → ~0.83 (dense blob); map-boundary slots count as non-interior.
  A long tendril has most edges on the boundary or facing other districts → **low** score. It is
  **tunable** via `threshold`. Valle Verde uses `0.35` as a *soft bonus* (`required: false`).
  → This is the mechanical lever for Scenario A: make compactness **`required: true` and tighten
  to ~0.42**, so an over-packing tendril *fails* where Valle Verde merely docked a bonus star.
- **The `compactness` criterion is min-across-districts** (`evaluate.ts:225-230`): it computes
  `Math.min(...perDistrictScores)` and passes only if the **worst** district clears `threshold`.
  This is what makes the over-pack wall (W2) real — one non-compact tendril drags the min down and
  fails the whole map. But it also makes winnability (W1) **strictly harder than Valle Verde's soft
  bonus**: *all five* districts must clear the threshold at once, together with pop-balance and the
  minority-share floor. The threshold therefore has a real ceiling — set it too high and no
  all-compact 5-district solution exists at all (W1 dies). The proof must land a value where the
  tendril fails **and** a clean all-compact map survives; that window, not a single number, is what
  gets pinned.
- **Scenario JSON schema** (from `scenario-005.json`): `rules {population_tolerance, contiguity}`;
  `success_criteria[] {id, required, description, criterion:{type,…}, character}`; `narrative
  {character{name,role,motivation}, intro_slides[{heading,body}], objective}`;
  `instigator_character`, `character_demographics`. Valle Verde already carries a *remediation*
  framing (court-appointed coordinator fixing a VRA violation) **and** a "simplified demographic
  data" disclaimer — both are templates to extend.

### The pedagogical arc (why these two, in this order)
- **Valle Verde (005)** establishes the **floor**: you *must* create a majority-minority district
  to cure cracking/dilution (Gingles). Compactness is a soft nicety.
- **The 55% Problem (010)** establishes the **ceiling**: you must *not* reach a racial target by
  over-concentrating into a bizarre shape (Bethune-Hill). Compactness becomes a hard gate.
- Together, floor + ceiling = the **narrow legally-defensible middle path** — the real
  two-failure zone.
- **The Proxy Problem (011)** moves to the **present**: after *Callais*, you must walk that path
  *without* looking at race — using proxies — because race-conscious lines are now suspect and
  partisan ones are unchallengeable.

## §APPROACH

### Key decision 1 — Scenario A is REMEDIATION, not temptation-resistance
A pass/fail *population-share* criterion gives a rational player **zero incentive to over-pack** —
they stop the instant a compact district clears the threshold. So the "55% danger" cannot be an
emergent temptation; it only teaches if **the starting map IS the court-struck over-packed
gerrymander** and the player's job is to *fix* it. The narrative must frame the player as fixing a
given illegal map (as Valle Verde already frames fixing a given diluted one) and must **never
imply the player resisted a temptation the mechanics never presented.** (DESIGN-013 line 60 already
specifies this starting map; we are sharpening the prose to match it.)

### Key decision 2 — the two-witness geometry proof is a HARD GATE
Scenario A's central claim ("you can't over-pack without breaking compactness, and you can't crack
without failing the majority") is **only true if the geometry forces the tradeoff**. Before any
narrative legal/mechanical claim is finalized, we must produce, over the *generated* JSON, both:
- **W1 — Winnability witness**: an assignment with one district ≥ `min_eligible_share` minority
  **and** every district's compactness ≥ the tightened threshold **and** population-balanced
  **and** contiguous. (Proves the middle path exists.)
- **W2 — Failure walls (both directions)**:
  - *Over-pack wall*: the greedy "grab every minority precinct" assignment (and the authored
    starting map) puts the target district ≥ threshold minority **but** compactness **< threshold**
    → `compactness` fails. **The authored starting map must fail compactness and nothing else** —
    district-count, population-balance, contiguity, and majority_minority all pass — so the single
    red criterion isolates the shape lesson.
  - *Crack wall*: spreading the community so no district reaches `min_eligible_share` →
    `majority_minority` fails.

If W1 and W2 cannot both be produced, the scenario name overclaims and the narrative is fiction —
**stop and re-tune geometry, do not write prose around it.** (Same feasibility-proof discipline
used for tutorial-004/006.) The compact winning district should land **just over** 0.50 — let the
proof find the achievable compact-max (0.52–0.53 is fine; don't hard-target 0.51 and fail on an
over-tight number). The "you didn't need 55%" lesson survives anywhere comfortably below 55%. The
extra minority precincts must be positioned so that grabbing them to chase 0.55+ *requires* a
non-compact reach.

### Key decision 5 — neutrality guardrails (DESIGN-014 + population-share honesty)
Every narrative beat must satisfy, and the draft appendix below is self-checked against:
1. **"The Court held X"** — never "wrongly/correctly held." Cite *Bethune-Hill*, *Callais* (2026),
   *Rucho* (2019), *Gingles* as **fact**, not indictment.
2. **Name the simplification** — the sim measures **population share**, not VAP/CVAP, and not
   whether the community can actually *elect* its candidate (no turnout, no bloc voting). A real
   court weighs **intent + shape**; here **compactness stands in for that judgment**. Say so.
3. **Do not claim the sim shows the minority *winning* a seat** — it shows a population threshold.
4. **Present both fairness arguments** — majority-minority districts as an essential remedy for
   vote dilution **vs.** sorting voters by race (even remedially) as what the Equal Protection
   Clause forbids. Take **no position**; hand the value-debate to the player in the debrief.
5. **De-loaded vocabulary** — no "loophole / trap / confession / getting away with it / colorblind
   trap / rigging / fair maps." Approved names stand: **"The 55% Problem"**, **"The Proxy
   Problem"** (DESIGN-014 line 86 replaced "The Colorblind Trap").
6. **Surface the EP-vs-VRA tension unresolved** — "the law has swung between these poles; this
   scenario shows the terrain both arguments fight over."
7. ***Callais* caution** — *Callais* is recent and, in our research, secondary-sourced. State its
   *holding* plainly (tightened illustrative-map standard; partisan explanation can defeat Gingles
   III; intent re-imported) but keep specifics conservative; **verify against a primary source
   before any beta exposure.**
8. **Compact is NOT a safe harbor** (the subtlest trap in this scenario). The mechanic makes
   non-compactness *the* failure signal, but *Bethune-Hill* explicitly moved *away* from the older
   Shaw-era intuition that a racial gerrymander must *look* bizarre: a compact district can still be
   a racial gerrymander if race predominated — predominance turns on **intent and process, not
   shape alone.** The prose must present non-compactness as *evidence* of a problem, and must
   **never** slide into "a compact district is safe / immune to challenge." Slide 3 and the debrief
   must say plainly: *a district doesn't have to look bizarre to be a racial gerrymander; courts
   weigh intent and process, not just shape — this scenario uses shape as a visible stand-in.* The
   mechanic can't carry this (compactness is our only lever), so the **narrative must.**

**Geometry recipe** (for the pipeline `spec.yaml`):
- A compact minority **core** that, drawn tightly, yields one district **just over 0.50** minority
  — the proof finds the achievable compact-max (0.52–0.53 is fine; discrete precincts + 10% balance
  + all-five-compact may not land exactly at 0.51) — with compactness ≥ threshold → W1.
- A scatter of additional minority precincts along a **corridor** away from the core, such that
  pulling them in to reach ~0.55+ forces a non-compact arm (compactness < 0.42) → over-pack wall.
- **Starting assignment** = the court-struck config: the target district is the over-packed tendril
  (compactness below threshold → fails). **It must fail compactness and *only* compactness** — the
  start map is contiguous, population-balanced, has the right district count, and the tendril is
  itself ≥ 0.50 minority (so `majority_minority` passes). One red criterion keeps the lesson
  unambiguous: the population is fine, the *shape* is the problem. Submitting unchanged loses on
  compactness alone. The player must pull the arm in — and not so far that the district drops below
  0.50 (crack wall).

**No new *engine* mechanics — one small additive *pipeline* extension** (verified this session via
a codebase-analyzer pass). The runtime engine is untouched: `loader.ts` (Invariants 3 & 7),
`evaluate.ts` (`majority_minority` = `computeDistrictGroupShares` over per-precinct
`demographic_groups` with a `{dimension,value}` `group_filter`), and `scenario.ts` already fully
support this — Valle Verde is the shipped proof. But the spec **pipeline** was only ever built to
emit *partisan* demographics (one group/precinct, `population_share: 1.0`, no `dimensions`, no
`group_schema`, and `mapCriterion` throws on `majority_minority`). So Scenario A needs the pipeline
taught to emit the ethnicity shape — the same *kind* of additive change as the `r_lte`/`r_gte`
ZoneFilter add (task 27), across exactly three files:
- `spec-types.ts` — a multi-group `dimension` + `groups[]` surface (each group: `value`,
  `id_suffix`, `name?`, its own `party_base` vote-lean + `turnout`), a per-zone `population_split`
  (where each ethnicity clusters — the geography), and `group_filter` on `CriterionSpec`.
- `demographics-stage.ts` — when `dimension`+`groups` are present, emit N `demographic_groups` per
  precinct (each tagged `dimensions:{[dimension]:value}`, shares summing to 1.0, **its own** vote
  lean + turnout — **option (ii)**, matching Valle Verde's racially-polarized-voting model) and set
  `group_schema` on the partial. Legacy single-group emission unchanged (opt-in on the new fields).
- `assembler.ts` — a `majority_minority` case in `mapCriterion`; `group_schema` threads through the
  existing `...partial` spread untouched.

**Option (ii) over (i)** (each ethnicity carries its own vote lean, vs. sharing the precinct's):
matches the sibling scenario (005), models the real VRA mechanism Scenario A is *about*, and is what
Scenario B (proxy inference) will need — build the capability once. A's *criteria* don't test the
vote outcome (`majority_minority` is pure population share, per slide 3's disclaimer), so this is
fidelity, not a gate — but authoring it right now avoids a throwaway. It also opens the door to
migrating Valle Verde itself off its last-Kotlin-holdout generator later.

### Scenario A — "The 55% Problem"

**character**
- name: "You"
- role: "Court-Appointed Redistricting Coordinator"
- motivation: "A federal court has struck down this county's map. To satisfy an old preclearance
  rule, the previous plan packed the community to a fixed 55% floor — reaching across the county
  with a narrow arm to hit that number. The court found that race predominated over compactness and
  community lines, making the district an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. You must redraw a
  district that still gives the community a fair opportunity to elect — built on compact, coherent
  geography, not a racial quota."

**intro_slides**
1. **"The 55% Floor"** — "Ten years ago, this county drew its swing district to be at least 55%
   [minority] — a fixed number, applied mechanically, justified as federal-preclearance insurance.
   To reach it, the district sends a long, narrow arm across the county to gather distant
   [minority] precincts."
2. **"Two Ways to Fail"** — "Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act says a sufficiently large,
   compact, and cohesive minority community must get a fair opportunity to elect its preferred
   candidate. The Supreme Court has also held that when a mapmaker makes race the *predominant*
   factor — subordinating compactness and community boundaries to hit a racial target — the
   district can be an unconstitutional racial gerrymander (*Bethune-Hill v. Virginia*). Draw the
   district too weak and you dilute the community's vote. Reach for a racial number with a bizarre
   shape and it's a racial gerrymander. The legal path runs between the two."
3. **"What This Map Can and Can't Show"** — "This scenario uses simplified data. It measures a
   district's [minority] **population share** — real Voting Rights Act analysis uses voting-age
   population and asks a harder question the game doesn't model: whether the community can actually
   elect its candidate, given who turns out and how people vote. And a real court weighs *intent*
   alongside shape; here, the compactness score stands in for that judgment. In real life a district
   doesn't have to *look* bizarre to be a racial gerrymander — courts weigh intent and process, not
   shape alone; this scenario uses shape as the part you can see. What's faithful is the **shape of
   the tradeoff**, not the exact numbers."

**objective**: "Redraw the district so the [minority] community keeps a fair opportunity to elect
(a district ≥ 50% [minority] population) — but on compact, coherent geography. Fix the racial
gerrymander without diluting the vote."

**debrief (post-win)**: "You found the middle path: a compact district that still gives the
community a majority — and notice you didn't need 55%. What made the old map vulnerable wasn't the
shape by itself; it was that a fixed racial number had become the *predominant* goal, with the
reaching shape as the visible evidence. That was the heart of *Bethune-Hill*: Virginia's mechanical
55% floor was struck down in 2018 because a lower majority — drawn from real communities and
geography rather than a racial quota — would have served the Voting Rights Act without making race
predominate. (Compactness isn't a guarantee: a district can be compact and *still* be a racial
gerrymander if race drove the pen; shape is just the part you can see.) Reasonable people still
disagree about all of this —
some see majority-minority districts as an essential remedy for the dilution of minority votes;
others argue that sorting voters by race, even to remedy past discrimination, is exactly what the
Equal Protection Clause forbids. The law itself has swung between these two principles over time.
This scenario doesn't take a side — it shows you the terrain both arguments are fighting over."
